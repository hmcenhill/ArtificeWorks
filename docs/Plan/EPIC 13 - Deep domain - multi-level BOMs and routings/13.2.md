## A component the factory makes: multi-level BOMs and the explosion

**Labels:** backend, domain

## Summary

Let a component be **manufactured rather than bought**: a nullable link from `Component` to the
`Product` that makes it turns the flat BOM into a tree. Add the recursive explosion (with a cycle
refusal and a depth cap), deepen `CatalogSeeder` so the three product lines genuinely share
sub-assemblies, and expose the tree on the existing product endpoint. **No behaviour changes** — a
made component that is in stock picks exactly as a bought one does.

## Why

The company's pitch is a shared platform: "a mine-spec Delver shares 70% of its bill of materials
with a hospitality Custodian". Today that is true but shallow — the shared parts are seven flat rows.
Real shared-platform engineering shares *assemblies*: the same control stack, the same power core,
built once and consumed by three products. That is the thing worth modelling, and it is what makes
13.4's commonality view more than a Venn diagram of screws.

Landing the model separately from the behaviour is deliberate. 13.3 is the story with pipeline
control flow in it; if the tree, the cycle guard and the deeper catalog land first and green, then
13.3 is about *one* new decision rather than about a new model and a new decision at once.

## Tasks

- [ ] **`Component` gains a nullable `MakeProductId`** (→ `products.ItemId`) meaning "this component
      is manufactured by that product; it is not bought". Bought components — the majority — keep it
      null and nothing about them changes. Add the inverse read where it is genuinely needed
      (a product's "what do I make?"), not reflexively. Migration `ManufacturedComponents`.
- [ ] **Guard the link at construction.** A component cannot be its own maker, and — the rule that
      matters — a product cannot appear inside its own explosion. Enforce what is cheap in the
      aggregate; the real guard is the explosion's cycle detection below, because a cycle can be
      created by two individually-legal edits.
- [ ] **`BomExplosion` (Domain).** Given a product and an order quantity, walk the tree to leaves and
      return both shapes callers need: the **tree** (nodes carrying component id/name, qty-per-parent,
      extended qty, depth, made/bought, and on-hand) and the **flattened leaf demand** (one row per
      raw component with total quantity). Refuses a cycle with a clear error naming the offending
      component; stops at a fixed `MaxDepth` (pick a small number — 4 or 5 — and state it in the
      code, since the catalog is two levels deep and a runaway is the failure mode that matters).
      Deterministic ordering, same `StringComparer.Ordinal` rule `ComputeDemand` follows.
- [ ] **`Product.ComputeDemand` stays exactly as it is.** It is the *one-level* demand — what picking
      draws off the shelf for this product — and after this story that is still the right answer,
      because a made component with stock is picked, not exploded. The explosion is a **reporting and
      planning** concern (13.4) and the input to 13.3's spawn decision. Do not quietly turn picking
      into a recursive planner here; that is the whole reason this story is separable.
- [ ] **Deepen `CatalogSeeder`.** Promote two shared-platform parts to sub-assemblies with their own
      products and BOMs, and make one of them **two levels deep**, so recursion is exercised by real
      seed data and not only by a test fixture. Suggested (adjust for flavour at story start):
      `CMP-CTRL-STACK` ← `SUBASM-CTRL-STACK` (governor linkage + logic drum + relay bank), and
      `CMP-CORE-AETHER` ← `SUBASM-CORE` (aether cell + regulator + `CMP-CASING-CORE`, itself made by
      `SUBASM-CORE-CASING`). Keep the seeder idempotent and keep on-hand levels for the made
      components non-zero, so the pipeline still runs end-to-end at the end of *this* story.
- [ ] **Keep the shared-platform claim honest and asserted.** `BillOfMaterialsTests` currently proves
      "7 of 10 components shared". After this story the claim should be stated at the level that is
      now true — shared *leaves* after explosion, and shared *sub-assemblies* — and asserted, not
      adjusted to whatever the new seed happens to produce. If the number moves, move the claim in the
      seeder's doc comment too; the pitch and the data must not drift.
- [ ] **Expose the tree.** Extend `GET /products/{id}` (or add `GET /products/{id}/bom?explode=true`
      — decide at story start; the existing DTO is already documented as "enough to eyeball the
      catalog") with the exploded tree plus each node's made/bought flag and on-hand. This is what
      13.4 renders, so shape it for that consumer: a tree the client can draw without a second call.
- [ ] **Tests.** Unit, mostly, and this is the story where they are the deliverable: explosion of a
      two-level product yields correct extended quantities; a cycle is refused and names the
      component; depth beyond the cap is refused; a product with no made components explodes to
      exactly its flat BOM (the regression that proves nothing changed for the other two lines);
      leaf demand aggregates a component reached by two different paths into one row. Integration:
      the seeded catalog explodes cleanly and the API returns the tree.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A component can be marked as made by a product, and the seeded catalog uses it at two levels
- [ ] Exploding a product returns both the tree and the aggregated leaf demand, correctly extended
      for an order quantity
- [ ] A cyclic BOM is refused with an error naming the cycle, and explosion is depth-capped
- [ ] The shared-platform overlap between the three lines is asserted by a test, at the level the
      deepened catalog makes true
- [ ] **The pipeline is unchanged**: an order for any of the three lines still flows Intake →
      Completed with no new stage, no new event, and no new hold

## Decisions (to confirm at story start)

- **The link lives on `Component`, not on `BomLine`.** "Is this made or bought?" is a property of the
  part, not of one product's use of it — the same control stack is made whoever calls for it. Putting
  it on the line would allow two products to disagree about how the same component comes to exist.
- **`bom_lines` is not touched.** No polymorphic line, no discriminator, no second FK. Every read path
  that resolves a BOM keeps working, and the explosion is a walk over the same rows.
- **Explosion is not planning.** It answers "what is this made of?", not "what should we buy?". The
  moment it starts subtracting on-hand and proposing orders it becomes MRP, which is a much larger and
  much less demoable thing. 13.3 makes the *pipeline* decide what to make, one shortage at a time.
- **Made components carry seeded stock.** The factory starts with sub-assemblies on the shelf. Without
  that, this story would break every order the moment it merged, and 13.3's spawn would be load-bearing
  before it is written.

## Notes

The cycle guard is the one piece of this story where a bug is dangerous rather than embarrassing:
combined with 13.3, a cyclic BOM is a work-order generator running against a shared public demo. Write
that test first.

If the API shape question (extend `GET /products/{id}` vs. a new sub-resource) is genuinely 50/50 at
story start, prefer the sub-resource — the flat BOM on the existing DTO is what the create form reads,
and an exploded tree arriving in that payload is a bigger response for every caller that only wanted
names.
